# Theory-claim fidelity: what a word-level check misses

The dimension list for the model-claim line of the source check in [`unit-plan-card-gate.md`](unit-plan-card-gate.md), "The send check" (trial). Read it when a sentence states a result of a model. Map the sentence against the hypotheses of the labelled result that controls it, along each dimension. Verdict: SUPPORTED, OVERSTATED or WRONG.

- Which object is unique: a per-case quantity or the equilibrium.
- Pointwise, average or optimized ("every patient" is pointwise).
- A fixed-parameter comparison or an equilibrium comparison; a ranking carries its existence condition.
- The verb matches the solution concept: "chooses" = the agent optimizes; "is determined by" = the closure sets it; "is a fixed parameter" = exogenous.
- A payment carries its feasibility condition; an action with a threshold is not automatic; a bound carries its conditions.
- A case the model's timing cannot represent is not a limit of a parameter.
- "Closed form" or "explicit" only for a formula; a root of an equation is "determined by a single equation".
- Entry, exit, adjustment or survival verbs only for dynamics the model contains. An imposed zero-profit condition identifies the level an activity can afford, not who enters or survives.
- Magnitude words ("huge", "substantially") only with a computed size; a strict inequality licenses direction only.

Repair in the paper's register: name the exact object the result delivers in one clause, keep the needed condition, drop the scaffolding. Never paste a stacked-qualifier replacement.

Why it matters: a sentence can read well, pass general readers, and still overstate its result. Such a failure usually sits on one of the dimensions above, which a word-level check does not test.
