# Building an academic paper from accepted research

Use this handbook to prepare a draft whose important information choices and explanations are resolved before an author walk. The patterns are conditional construction aids, not sentence slots. A nearly finished draft may still expose a genuine author choice; it should not require the author to supply missing logic repeatedly.

## 1. Decide what the reader must understand

A paper should let its intended reader recover its question, supported answer, reasoning and consequential limits, and understand its contribution relative to the literature. It must also permit verification. Convincing prose with an unsupported link and an inventory of correct statements both fail this combined task.

| Scale | Construction decision | Evidence of success | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | What supported answer constitutes the contribution? | Introduction, results, evidence and conclusion describe the same comparison and limits | Attractive pitch stronger than the research, or disconnected findings |
| Section | What distinct question is answered here? | The section supplies its answer and prepares what follows | Proof order mistaken for reading order; overlapping section jobs |
| Connected unit | What must be read together to assess this claim? | Preparation, result and explanation make a coherent argument | A good sentence separated from its premise or trade-off |
| Paragraph | What changes in the reader's understanding here? | Sentences develop a definition, comparison, mechanism, evidence claim or transition | True facts collected around a topic |
| Sentence | Who does what, relative to what, under what conditions? | Actor, verb, referent, unit and inferential force are recoverable | Unanchored pronoun, hidden comparison, unsupported “therefore” |

“Intended understanding” includes likely implications, not only literal correctness. A paragraph about lower aggregate employment must not invite the reader to conclude that each remaining worker earns less unless that claim is established. Emphasis cannot license a stronger inference than the evidence.

## 2. Separate four orders

**Scientific dependency order:** what has been established, with which definitions, assumptions and proofs. Preserve it when deciding what can be claimed.

**Exposition order:** what the reader must know at each point. Choose the question and promised statement first, then identify indispensable teaching prerequisites. A proof helper does not automatically belong before the main result.

**Drafting order:** how to produce the argument efficiently. Central results and explanations may be settled before the final introduction. An early pitch can expose a gap without becoming final prose.

**Review order:** which risks and dependencies to examine first. Architecture or changed scientific scope may invalidate dependent prose; a pronoun repair usually needs only a local check.

If exposition reveals an unsupported mechanism, route it to scientific review. Do not solve the gap by stronger language. If one unresolved question does not affect another section, continue independent work there.

## 3. Build in this sequence

### Recover the actual task

Read the current manuscript, reviewed research, author decisions and successful existing passages. Recover audience, venue, contribution, scope, protected material and delivery authority. Ask only for genuinely missing choices.

Separate scientific constraints from author choices, routine editorial judgment and unresolved questions. A new model convention is a scientific decision. A newly preferred section order is a presentation decision. Do not describe either retrospectively as a failure to follow a rule that did not yet exist.

### Set the paper's promise

Write a short working account of the question, supported answer, explanation or identification argument, closest intellectual comparison and consequence. This is a design aid, not mandatory manuscript text.

Map results to the promise they discharge. A full characterization owes more than a headline comparative static. A global optimum needs the objective, domain and optimum; its value formula earns space only if it serves the reader's question. A mechanism claim needs evidence that supports that mechanism, not simply the outcome.

### Assign section jobs and interfaces

For each section, identify the incoming question, distinct answer and objects used next. Inspect joins: does the setup prepare the result actually stated? Do empirical predictions match the theory's measure and domain? Does the conclusion preserve claim strength?

“Model, results, evidence” is a topic list. “How the institution changes incentives; what behavior follows in equilibrium; what observable comparison can test the implication” is an argument map. Print a roadmap only when the reader needs it.

### Select the statement before its prerequisites

Decide which conclusions the statement must deliver. Then distinguish objects needed to parse it, economic steps needed to understand it, and helpers used only in its proof. Do not retain an optional formula merely because its supporting notation has already been introduced.

### Prepare the natural unit

The unit is the smallest connected piece whose editorial decision can be assessed honestly: perhaps a sentence, a paragraph with a rule in its footnote, or a proposition with orientation and explanation. It is not a fixed line count.

For a new, uncertain or materially changed unit, record a few lines about its question, selected information, logical relation, important inference and protected scope. A routine wording repair can reuse the existing decision. Do not add a design approval meeting when no author choice remains.

### Draft from the best available prose

Keep sound existing material. Use paragraph boundaries for changes in reader question, economic state or comparison. Connected causal or conditional clauses can form one thought; “one thought per sentence” is not a ban on reasoning.

Read the complete unit at reading pace without internal notes. Locate missing actors, overloaded contrasts, unexplained symbols, abrupt object changes and repeated setup. Repair the cause rather than enforcing a word count.

### Review and assemble

Use the [review protocol](review-protocol.md). Repair purpose, selection or order before polishing wording when those are the failed layers. Recheck the affected joins. Before declaring a paper ready, read the assembled paper for promises, consistency, figures, captions and references. Do not repeat a whole-paper audit after every local edit.

## 4. Paragraph patterns by purpose

| Purpose | Useful construction | Emphasis | Omit or vary when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motivation | Concrete activity or friction → why its design matters → question | The consequential problem | An institutional tour would repeat familiar context |
| Puzzle | Grounded expectation → countervailing observation or force → unresolved issue | A real tension | The contribution is a characterization, not a surprise |
| Contribution | What the paper does → supported difference → consequence | Intellectual increment | Detailed result previews already do the teaching |
| Model overview | Essential actors and institution → interacting choices → varied input | Isolated economic problem | A cast list repeats the formal setup |
| Institutional mechanism | Actor/action → rule, timing or information → consequence | Relevant constraint | The sentence belongs to the model rather than institutional facts |
| Worked operation | Initial quantities → action/payment → new state → implication | Easily confused distinction | Calculation teaches nothing new |
| Assumption | Economic regime → exact restriction | Class of settings studied | A gloss would imply a theorem not established by that restriction alone |
| Definition | Objects → optimality and consistency conditions | Precise solution concept | Solved behavior is being smuggled into the definition |
| Section opening | Current question, with only needed connection to prior material | Distinct task | The previous passage already supplies orientation |
| Pre-result orientation | Economic problem → exact object/comparison → indispensable definitions | What is being solved | All three are already clear; then add nothing |
| Formal statement | Premises/domain → complete promised conclusions | Result content | An arbitrary part count or fixed theorem length distorts grouping |
| Mechanism | Relevant choice/constraint → supported response → consequence | Why the finding holds | The relation is a definition, identity or association, not a mechanism |
| Bridge | Needed connection → next question | Relationship between units | The connection is obvious |
| Literature comparison | Closest work's result → precise difference → consequence | What changes understanding | A compact comparison is becoming a tutorial |
| Empirical prediction | Theory object → observable counterpart → relation/scope | What the test reveals | Proxy cannot support the proposed interpretation |
| Empirical design | Variation → comparison → identifying condition → estimand | What evidence establishes | Emotional emphasis or result preview obscures identification |
| Empirical result | Question → meaningful estimate/pattern and uncertainty → interpretation | Finding | A fixed statistic quota replaces judgment |
| Qualification/footnote | Relevant limit or rule where interpretation needs it | Preventing a material false inference | It is procedural reassurance or duplicate caveat |
| Conclusion | Answer → changed understanding → warranted implication | Durable contribution | It recreates the abstract or introduces unsupported results |

These patterns can combine when their jobs fit. They are not mandatory paragraphs. A definition may succeed through neutral precision; it does not need a dramatic final sentence.

## 5. Sentences, links and emphasis

Select the relationship among necessary pieces: cause, contrast, definition, sequence, qualification or parallel evidence. Do not draw causal arrows simply to make a paragraph look organized.

Choose an opening from what the reader needs at that point. A result paragraph may begin with the finding; an unfamiliar institution may need actor and operation first. A pre-result paragraph can ask the question without explaining the answer twice.

Use subjects and verbs that expose agency. Check pronouns after sentences containing several actors. “This effect” must refer to an identifiable effect. Stable terminology helps more than forced synonyms.

Attach each condition to the conclusions it governs. A common premise can govern several conclusions, but a new sentence must not accidentally lose that scope. A local condition belongs with its own clause. A footnote may carry secondary detail, but the main assertion must remain truthful before the note is reached.

“Therefore,” “because,” “despite” and similar links carry claims. Check them against evidence. Name an identity as an identity. Evidence consistent with a mechanism does not automatically identify it.

Let emphasis follow the contribution. Do not bury the result in a subordinate clause while giving an optional formula its own display and heading. End when the paragraph's job is complete, not with a grander repetition of its first sentence.

## 6. Decide what earns space

Ask both: **what would disappear with this item, and what does it cost the reader here?** This prevents over-trimming and true-but-misplaced inventories. Later citation counts are supporting evidence about use, not a test of economic relevance.

A benchmark belongs when the question compares with it, a welfare/deviation argument requires it, or it gives essential interpretation. Do not introduce one solely to report an optional height formula.

Keep examples that teach an operation, distinguish confusing quantities or convey an important magnitude. A witness proving that assumptions can hold may belong in a proof appendix rather than the main explanation.

A figure needs a reader question before a format: state transitions, available decisions, realized timing, a quantitative relationship, empirical uncertainty. One clear job usually works better than an omnibus diagram. Inspect final-scale output and ask a fresh reader what it shows. An illustrative path is not a guaranteed outcome; a fixed-arrival diagram is not itself a probability proof.

Necessary scope stays even when brevity is desired. A common sufficient domain can be useful without printing each conclusion's widest possible domain. Do not erase a hypothesis or falsely imply universal validity.

A self-contained model must state its operative rules in the main paper, including main-text footnotes where suitable. The appendix proves and supports; it is not a dumping ground for every cut paragraph. Verify promised proofs, corner cases and empirical support. Before deleting duplication, identify the surviving home and check it after integration.

## 7. Published exemplars

Find actual published passages doing the same job, including the relevant preparation and following explanation. Compare complete units. Read a proposition when redesigning a proposition; an opening alone cannot teach full-profile construction.

Record consequential construction choices, whether they transfer, and how material gaps are resolved. A benchmark indispensable to one paper's convergence question need not belong in a different paper. Do not import an exemplar's economics or imitate its word count.

Duffie, Gârleanu and Pedersen (2005), “Over-the-Counter Markets,” is one bibliographic starting point for economics. Obtain the text and inspect the relevant passage before using it as evidence. Other fields and jobs need other exemplars. The package includes no published paper text.

## 8. Worked demonstrations

All examples below are invented teaching cases. Their stated assumptions are part of each example, not general claims about real institutions.

### A. A short definition worth retaining

**Candidate:** “Each supplier chooses a delivery quantity before observing demand. The buyer pays the posted unit price for each delivered unit.”

**Decision:** Retain if these are the reviewed rules. Actor, choice, information timing and payment base are explicit. Adding a crescendo or an unnecessary contrast would make the definition worse. Shortening it to “suppliers choose quantities and receive payment” would lose the information and price rules.

### B. Pre-result orientation without explaining the result twice

**Context:** Capacity, costs and an arrival rate are already defined; the next result characterizes optimal capacity.

**Overloaded:** “Capacity rises because more arrivals increase revenue and firms respond by expanding, an important force that the proposition below demonstrates.”

**Better orientation:** “We next ask how the optimal capacity responds to the arrival rate.”

**Why:** It supplies the new question. The proposition carries the precise domain and conclusion; the following explanation develops the supported revenue-versus-cost argument. If the preceding sentence already asks this question, omit the orientation too.

### C. A full profile cannot become only its headline

**Promised result:** Equilibrium of a market with supplier quantities, a buyer's price rule and entry.

**Incomplete:** “Equilibrium capacity increases above a demand threshold.”

**Repair:** The formal unit must identify each strategy or import an already established component by exact reference, and state how entry/clearing determines remaining quantities, including relevant zero-entry cases. Then its discussion can emphasize the threshold.

**Why:** The comparative-static headline is not the equilibrium profile. Do not invent a particular formula merely to make the template look complete.

### D. State a sufficient common domain once

**Reviewed inputs:** Outcome A improves above one cutoff; outcome B improves above a possibly larger cutoff. The paper's question is where both improve together.

**Clean statement:** “There is a finite threshold such that, above it, outcome A improves and outcome B improves.”

**Why:** The common domain governs both conclusions. If a later sentence says “Outcome B improves” without carrying the domain, it may falsely look universal. The wider separate domain is optional support unless the main question needs it. “A threshold” does not claim the smallest threshold.

### E. Separate duration from occupancy

**Reviewed input:** A service interval's length decreases, but the frequency of intervals can change.

**Ambiguous:** “Customers spend less time in service.”

**Repair:** “Each service interval becomes shorter.”

**Why:** The repair states the measured object. It does not imply that a customer's fraction of time receiving service falls. Add the latter distinction only if the context otherwise creates that inference; do not repeat it after every duration sentence.

### F. Explain a ratio with proportional comparisons

**Teaching identity:** Productivity is output divided by labor hours.

**Wrong inference:** “Output rises, so productivity rises.”

**Repair:** “Productivity rises if output grows proportionally faster than labor hours.”

**Why:** Direction alone does not sign a ratio. The identity is useful here because it teaches the exact comparison. It is not a causal theory of why output or hours change.

### G. Distinguish population outcomes

**Invented finding:** A policy reduces the number of participating firms; surviving firms' average revenue rises.

**Bad summary:** “Large gains for survivors come at the expense of all other firms.”

**Repair:** “Fewer firms participate, while average revenue among participating firms rises.”

**Why:** The repair names population and measure without asserting an income transfer or losses for every nonparticipant. A distributional mechanism needs separate evidence.

### H. An empirical result with its denominator

**Invented source:** A randomized intervention changes mean completion from 40% to 44%; the estimated difference is four percentage points with a stated confidence interval.

**Bad:** “The intervention increases completion by 4%.”

**Repair:** “The intervention increases completion by four percentage points, from 40% to 44%,” followed by the relevant uncertainty when reporting the estimate.

**Why:** A four-point change is a 10% relative increase from the baseline. Keep the estimand, sample and uncertainty appropriate to the actual source. This arithmetic example is not a finding about a real intervention.

### I. Move a proof helper without losing the argument

**Situation:** A result gives the location of an optimum. Its proof defines an auxiliary function; the inherited main statement also prints an optional value formula using that function.

**Repair:** Decide whether the value answers a reader question. If it does not, remove that optional display from the main result, retain the auxiliary function in the proof, and check every dependent reference. Keep the optimum's domain and uniqueness claim intact.

**Why:** Selecting the result first avoids manufacturing prerequisites. The opposite error is deleting an economically meaningful benchmark merely because it has no later citation.

## 9. Review checkbook

Use the rows that apply. A completed checklist does not substitute for reading the argument.

| Test | Question | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | What does the reader learn, and why here? | Joined passage and section promise |
| Fidelity | Is every changed claim supported at its actual scope? | Exact reviewed source and context |
| Logical chain | Does each necessary link exist and carry the right force? | Actor/action/comparison sequence |
| Selection | Is a true detail relevant; is a deleted detail still needed? | Reader job and surviving homes |
| Scope | Do premises govern all intended clauses? | Statement plus surrounding maintained conditions |
| Measures | Are stocks, flows, shares, averages, horizons and populations distinct? | Definitions, source cells and labels |
| Reader | Can an unprimed reader recover the important meaning? | Actual publication-facing packet and response |
| Exemplar | Does a matched published unit reveal a consequential construction gap? | Actual side-by-side complete units |
| Joins | Did the change invalidate neighboring or queued material? | Preceding/following passages and consumers |
| Delivery | Does the real artifact contain the approved version? | Render, references, figures, markup and destination |
| Learning | Did the correction change the responsible next decision? | Actual next-use input and output |

Stop when material findings are resolved and the authorized deliverable is usable. Reopen an affected check for a semantic change, unresolved finding, unreliable evidence or explicit requirement, not just to collect another PASS.
