# Introduction, setting and empirical exposition

These are purpose patterns, not mandatory subsection counts. Read the actual sources and current author decisions before drafting. Preserve strong existing prose and protected coauthor material.

## Introduction

Start from the contribution: a supported question and answer, the reason the answer matters, and the closest intellectual comparison. The introduction should teach enough of the institution and model or design for a broad intended reader to understand the findings. It should not reproduce every model rule or empirical table.

| Job | Reader need | Common repair |
|---|---|---|
| Motivation | Concrete stakes and the question | Put the economically important activity before a catalogue of technical details |
| Contribution | What changes relative to known results | Replace an exhaustive preview with the precise intellectual increment |
| Model overview | Actors, choices, essential rule, varied input | Separate cast, timing and outcome when compression obscures them |
| Theory finding | What happens, why and under what consequential scope | Retain the needed teaching step; remove proof-defense scaffolding |
| Evidence | Observable comparison, finding, magnitude and uncertainty | State the point before numbers; retain denominators and sample |
| Literature | Closest result and exact difference | Avoid turning a compact contribution comparison into a tutorial |

A question-led opening is useful when it asks the paper's real question. A puzzle must have a grounded expectation, not an invented reader belief. A closing roadmap is optional; avoid repeated inline section navigation through every result paragraph.

When an author wants emphasis stronger than the current evidence, preserve its legitimate purpose in the nearest supported form. If the desired conclusion itself is unsupported, explain the gap and decision rather than quietly substituting a different contribution.

## Institutional setting

Teach the facts this paper's question needs: actors and operations, relevant layers of design, rule and timing, scale or variation, and the obstacle the research addresses. Distinguish sourced institutional facts, sourced incentives, model choices, results and empirical interpretations.

Plan the learning sequence independently of the inherited paragraph order. A flat factual catalogue and a preview of the model's results are opposite failures. Give each paragraph a job: environment, landscape, mechanism, worked operation, evolution or transition.

A worked example should state initial quantities, action, payment, resulting state and implication. Verify arithmetic and distinguish a paid average price from a subsequent marginal quote when relevant. Familiar analogies need an exact mapping and a boundary.

Use figures only when they make the operation or relation easier to understand. Explain each panel where its mechanism is discussed. Keep schematic claims scoped; a representative case does not establish the entire institutional landscape.

End on the unresolved economic choice the model studies. Do not smuggle a modeling convention or solved comparative static into the factual section.

## Empirical section

Before writing, recover the theory predictions or empirical questions, observable measures, data definitions, sample rules, variation, identification and uncertainty. The opening can list the few distinct questions the section tests when that improves orientation. Explain how each test relates to a main-text result; a central prediction should not be intelligible only through an appendix pointer.

Build each unit around its purpose:

1. The question and observable comparison.
2. Design and identifying condition, where not already taught.
3. Result with relevant magnitude, denominator, horizon and uncertainty.
4. Interpretation at the evidence's actual strength.

Do not force all four into separate paragraphs. Use prior setup once it exists. Do not imply that a proxy is the latent mechanism, that consistent evidence excludes alternatives, or that a gross-fee effect is a net-income effect.

Tables and figures should answer a question, not display every available estimate. Define sample, units and uncertainty in the text/caption where needed. Put promised supporting evidence in the appendix with accurate references. A source editor must check that a changed theory statement still supports empirical predictions cited to it.

## Experiment or pipeline explanation

State the question, what enters the pipeline, what each material operation changes, and what comes out. Use a concrete input/output example when the operation is unfamiliar. Keep implementation details only when they affect meaning, reproducibility or the reader's decision. An impressive sequence of method names is not an explanation.

## Referee or technical assessment

Explain what the paper does, the particular concern, the evidence for it and why it affects the claim. Preserve relevant defenses and contrary evidence. Separate demonstrated error from interpretation and unresolved question. A formula reference is an anchor, not a standalone explanation.

Use one concern per connected comment, with the evidence needed to evaluate it. Do not add a simulation, numerical example or requested test merely to fill a pattern. A warmer revision must preserve the substantive standard and conclusion; a shorter revision must preserve necessary reasoning. Keep portal/submission rules outside the prose procedure.
