# Plain language and empty rhetorical patterns

Prefer concrete objects, stable technical terms and verbs that show what happens. Plainness preserves distinctions; it does not replace a precise scientific term with a vague everyday word. Short sentences alone do not create a clear argument.

## Diagnose the reader problem

- **Manufactured drama:** an invented belief, dismissive contrast or grand reveal postpones the actual claim. State the positive explanation unless a real misunderstanding needs correcting.
- **Forced symmetry:** balanced slogans, neat counts and mirrored clauses hide unequal domains, actors or mechanisms. Let the scientific relationship determine grouping.
- **Decorative explanation:** metaphors, vague importance claims or agency assigned to an estimator replace a real link. Name the object, action and warranted consequence.
- **Repeated navigation:** preview, question, recap and caveat all do the same job. Keep the one needed at this location.
- **Nominalization chains:** abstract nouns obscure who chooses, observes or pays. Restore the actor and verb.
- **False concision:** deletion removes the denominator, timing distinction or intermediate step the reader needs. Restore meaning instead of optimizing length.

These are editorial cues, not tests of AI authorship. A useful question, contrast or qualification can look superficially similar. Inspect the whole unit before deleting it. Do not turn cues into a banned-word score or compulsory extra scan.

## Constructed repairs and controls

The examples below are invented for instruction and have no empirical status.

| Candidate | Repair or decision |
|---|---|
| “This is not about saving money. It is about transforming operations.” | Name the supported objective: “The system reduces the time staff spend reconciling orders.” If saving money is also established, state that separately with its evidence. |
| “Two queues. One bottleneck.” | For a technical heading, “The shared processing constraint” may orient better. Retain an actual queue explanation; a slogan does not teach the constraint. |
| “The policy unlocks participation, highlighting the importance of incentives.” | Supply the particular link if sourced: “The subsidy lowers the cost of applying, so more eligible firms apply.” Keep its identification and population scope. |
| “Workers gain; firms lose.” | State measured outcomes: “Wages rise while the number of participating firms falls,” if that is the actual finding. Quantity decline does not itself establish lower profit per firm or a transfer. |
| “Our estimator reveals the true dynamics.” | Name what is estimated and its identifying assumptions. No estimator has unrestricted access to truth. |
| “Why does the policy change entry?” | Keep if this introduces the section's actual question and the following unit answers it. Questions are not inherently defective. |
| “Average visits become shorter, although the share of time visiting need not fall.” | Keep when that distinction is necessary. If already taught and no new ambiguity arises, remove only the repeated copy. |
| “Although the treatment effect is positive, its interval includes zero.” | Keep when supported. This is a consequential uncertainty statement, not gratuitous hedging. |

A repair should identify the information or inference it improves, not merely the removed word. Read the changed join and retain a useful opposite example. A good next draft is better evidence of learning than a longer blacklist.

## Prose choices

Use a full actor name at the start of a paragraph or when the subject changes. Use pronouns once their antecedents are immediate and unique. Do not replace excessive pronouns with mechanical repetition of long names in every sentence.

Put scope where it governs the claim; inherit clearly established scope where safe. Repeating “holding everything else fixed” after every sentence adds burden without precision. Removing the only scope clause changes the claim.

Use one ordinary or canonical name consistently. A new term earns its cost by distinguishing a necessary concept. Do not teach a new codename for an interval that “duration” would describe clearly.

Vary sentence length naturally. Connect causal or conditional clauses that belong together. Split when the reader's question or comparison changes. No word band, adjective count or imitation of an author's rhythm certifies quality.
