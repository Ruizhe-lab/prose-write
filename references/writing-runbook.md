# From “go ahead and write” to delivery

Use one accountable editorial lead, one canonical writer for each file or hunk, and independent assessment suited to the task. These are functions, not a compulsory team size or choice of model. A small task may combine lead and writer; its self-review is still not independent review.

## 1. Assign clear ownership

| Function | Responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial lead | Author intent, paper promise, joined argument, review reconciliation and readiness | Presents the usable draft and real decisions; does not certify science through author approval |
| Canonical writer/integrator | Exact files, application, cross-references, figures, builds and authorized delivery | No competing writer edits the same canonical hunk silently |
| Source/editorial reviewer | Scientific fidelity, chain, information selection, order and task fit | Can challenge the proposed design; does not inherit manuscript ownership |
| Fresh reader | Understanding, expectations and consequential confusion | Sees publication-facing prose and necessary prior context only |
| Scientific owners and reviewers | New claims, assumptions, proof changes and required certification | Writing convenience does not widen scientific authority |
| Author | Material authorial choices and explicitly reserved decisions | Routine execution should not be returned as a new permission question |

Independent tasks can run in parallel: later-unit preparation, source checking, figure work, appendix reconciliation and learning. Give each a bounded scope, version, deliverable, owner and acceptance criteria. Serialize real dependencies and conflicting writes. A handoff is complete when custody or actual action is observed, not merely when a brief file exists.

## 2. Keep one current project record

Use the existing brief or walk record. It should make the current unit, candidate version, scientific contract, author decisions, material review findings, request dispositions, application state and next action recoverable. Do not create a different bureaucracy for every correction, approval and handoff.

Keep distinct statuses: scientifically supported, editorially reviewed, author-approved, applied, rendered and published. Approval of a caption is not approval of its art. A correct source does not establish good placement. A successful build does not establish that a remote viewer received it.

A changed decision invalidates its dependent work, not the whole project. Reassess queued passages when their scientific scope, preceding context, statement selection or current author choice changes. A local punctuation repair does not reopen an unrelated section. Version hashes can detect changed declared bytes; only judgment can identify semantic dependencies.

## 3. Recover before asking

Read the current project and reviewed sources. Separate:

- **Agent work:** finding a source, checking a term, locating a rule, comparing builds.
- **Editorial judgment:** selecting an example, improving order, combining fragments, removing repetition within authority.
- **Author/scientific choices:** changed contribution, audience, scientific claim, model convention, or explicitly reserved presentation choice.

Settle the first two. For the third, prepare supported alternatives, consequences and a recommendation. Continue unaffected work while that choice is pending. Do not turn intake into a standard questionnaire or assume a completed paper is reopened by a stale brief.

## 4. Prepare the joined draft

Follow the handbook: scientific contract → paper promise and section jobs → statement/content selection → necessary teaching prerequisites → connected unit → prose.

For a new or materially changed unit, record briefly what question it answers, which facts discharge it, why the order fits, and which inference must survive. Reuse sound accepted decisions for local changes. An independent reviewer may reject the design; filled fields are not evidence of quality.

Present the first author-facing version only after material source/editorial and reader findings are reconciled. Include the whole decision unit: a proposition with its orientation and discussion, or an unchanged cost paragraph together with the changed benefit paragraph. Do not make the author reconstruct the retained neighbor from memory.

Prepare and check the whole agreed walkthrough before the author begins, whether it covers a passage, a section or the paper. The author should be able to move through the prepared material without waiting for the next piece to be drafted. Bring a choice that genuinely requires author steering as soon as its supporting material is ready, and continue unaffected preparation while it is pending. Preserve the author's chosen scope and cadence.

## 5. Review and repair

Use [review protocol](review-protocol.md). Give informed reviewers the purpose and sources. Give the unprimed reader only the prose and real prior context. Keep those inputs distinct even if the platform offers several agents under one model.

Resolve findings by evidence and consequence. A source reviewer may correctly challenge a causal attribution while proposing wording that loses the passage's purpose. The lead must retain the legitimate purpose in its nearest supported form. If the conclusion itself lacks support, expose the scientific decision instead of disguising it.

Repair at the failed layer: science, purpose, selection, order, explanation, wording, source route or delivery. Preserve accepted material that still works. Recheck the affected claims and joins. No default count of clean rounds, objections or reviewers certifies prose quality. Separate scientific proof requirements still apply.

## 6. Conduct the author walk without blocking it

Present a short orientation and then connected passages at the author's chosen cadence. Show complete old/new text when requested. Answer substantive questions explicitly before presenting dependent rewrites. A request for an explanation is not discharged by a new paragraph.

Separate distinct requests in one message. “Use this, understand why, and apply it elsewhere” contains local acceptance, learning and propagation work. A later “continue” does not approve unseen text. Record each disposition without asking the author to do administrative work.

Let the canonical writer apply accepted edits and build while the lead discusses the next prepared unit. Detailed retrospectives, source hunts that do not affect the next decision, figure adjustments and skill updates stay in the background. Pause the next passage only for a concrete dependency that would make it misleading, or an explicit author request to pause.

A repeated objection is a signal to reconsider the responsible layer. Stop generating nearby synonyms when the real problem is the wrong paragraph job, missing comparison or wrong section order.

## 7. Figures and appendices

Give a figure a reader question before deciding its layout. Inspect the actual art with caption and relevant preceding text at its final scale. A clean figure may still teach the wrong causal or timing story. Keep illustrative realizations distinct from general results.

Match appendix material to retained claims, proofs, boundary cases and promised empirical support. Do not store every deleted paragraph there. Keep operative model rules available in the main paper when it promises a self-contained model; a main-text footnote can be the sole main-paper home. Preserve exact scientific scope and check the destination of moved material.

## 8. Deliver and recover

Verify the actual assembled output: approved text, figures, captions, references, visible markup and destination. If remote publication is authorized, verify the remote revision; a local file or old screenshot is insufficient. Use only the authority applicable to that action. Routine execution within an explicit delegation does not need permission again.

On interruption, resume from exact files, accepted decisions, pending changes, unresolved findings and active owners. Reuse valid checks. Do not recreate accepted prose or replay the entire author walk. If evidence is missing, state the localized uncertainty.

## 9. Learn during the work

Apply [learning](learning.md) on corrections and completed work units. Keep project choices local and reusable craft guidance at one canonical home. A completion may conclude “no new general lesson.” Observe the next relevant use before claiming transfer.

The optional [packet helper](helper-guide.md) prepares inspectable files and detects declared changes. It sends nothing, launches no reviewer and judges no prose. A saved brief still needs delivery through the platform's internal worker route. Never route an internal agent task through an author-input or approval tool.

## 10. Measure the intended outcome

Observe real first-author exposures: material scientific errors, required reconstruction, repeated correction classes, avoidable waiting and preserved accepted content. Distinguish unavoidable new author choices from preparation failures. Do not drop difficult units after seeing the outcome, or infer a success percentage from historical exercises and synthetic fixtures.

The aim is fewer repeated corrections and better usable drafts, not a longer instruction corpus or more receipts.
