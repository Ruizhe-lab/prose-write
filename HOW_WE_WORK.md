# How we work

The writing team and how it works with the author. Each decision is stated once, here or in the file named.
A row that names a principle in capitals rests on that principle, stated under "Principles". Every other row is a default. The author's word replaces it.
"The unit file" is [references/unit-plan-card-gate.md](references/unit-plan-card-gate.md).

## What the author does

1. Says what to work on. Says "prepare" to read it finished later.
2. Agrees the one-minute plan of each section: its point and the order of its parts.
3. For each part, picks among the versions shown, mixes them, or corrects. How many versions: "One message" below.
4. Says yes once to delivering approved text, and yes once at the end to sign it off.
5. Sends anything that leaves the team.
6. Never briefs the partner, carries text between writers, runs a command, repeats a decision already on record, or waits while a writer drafts.

## The seats

Seats are functions. A seat may be a person or an AI assistant.

| Seat | Who | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | "Live or prepared" below says which writer. | The plan, Version 1, the mix, the send check, the message to the author, the record. |
| Partner | One standing partner writer, from a different model family or a different person. | Its own plan and version, written without opening the lead's. A check of the lead's draft. The audit of a coauthor's section. |
| Fresh reader | A new reader with no history of the text, started by the lead. | One blind read (the unit file). |
| Fidelity reader | A reader who wrote none of the text (the unit file says which). | One read of a prepared whole against its sources. |
| Helper | A small assistant or colleague, briefed in writing by the lead. | Script runs, source lookups, searches. Apply, build, deliver, prove delivery. |
| Coordinator | For a whole grant application only: a person or assistant briefed by the lead. | Files, budget, tracker, portal draft ([the package file](references/grant-package.md)). |
| Mathematics author | Whoever the host project names for formal work. | Every new or changed symbol, formula and formal statement, as old/new pairs. |
| Outside reviewer | One qualified reader outside the team. | "The outside review" below. |
| The author | The person whose text it is. | Scope, each plan, each part. The author's alone: the arc, the contribution, keep or cut, section architecture, final say on a term, a new task. |

## The loop for one part

```
the author's words + the agreed plan
     |                      |
LEAD, in the author's chat   PARTNER, in its own file
own card + version           own card + version
     |___ each checks the other's draft ___|
                  |
LEAD: send check on every version; fresh reader where due
                  |
ONE MESSAGE: current text, the versions (three for a live part), what was checked, one recommendation
                  |
THE AUTHOR picks, mixes or corrects -> record; partner told the same turn
                  |
HELPER, in the background: apply, build, deliver, prove delivery
Both writers already have the next part drafted.
```

## Live or prepared

The channel a job came through never sets the mode. Use the first row that fits.

| Question | Case | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Mode | The author says "prepare", "later", "while I am away", or the job says the author is away | Prepared (PREPARE-THEN-WALK). |
| Mode | The author can answer you now | Live. Do not ask. (LEAD-WRITES) |
| Mode | No live contact with the author: the job came from another person or process | Prepared. Say so in the first line. |
| Lead | The author is talking to one writer, at any point in a job | That writer, from then. It writes, and resumes from the record. (LEAD-WRITES) |
| Lead | A job handed over by another person or process | The writer who receives it. A writer briefed as partner stays the partner. |
| Messages | Live | The conversation the author is in. |
| Messages | Prepared | Where the job says. Otherwise each message goes back to whoever handed over the job, in full, with the first line "not yet shown to the author". It is passed on unchanged. |

- No writer relays: none carries the author's words to another writer and the draft back while the author waits. Handing over a finished, checked message is not a relay.
- The partner never decides the mode. Its brief names it, or it asks the lead before writing.

### Prepared work

These steps are for a text the author approves part by part. An audit: only its findings message. An email: only its sheet.

1. The first message holds the plan of every section in the job (the unit file), or what the sheet puts in its place. It states the deadline and the date the author will have the text. If that waits on an answer: the days needed after it, and the last day an answer still meets the deadline.
2. Both writers write every part. Each part's queue sheet holds all three versions. Each part passes the send check.
3. Build a reading copy. A shared repository: the whole document in the job's working copy, each part's recommended version in pending markup (for example red text). It is not applied text and is never delivered. No repository (grant, letter): the file the author reads, no markup.
4. Run the checks of a prepared whole (the unit file).
5. Put the reading copy where the author reads, and confirm it opens there.
6. Send one ready notice per job: goal, step, what is ready and where, the plan it rests on and whether the author agreed it, open decisions, what was checked.
7. The author reads the copy and stops where they want. What they then see: "One message", the reading-copy row. Nothing is regenerated.

## First, find the project and the text

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Which project | The author's words name it. They win over a brief and over an open record. Two matches or none: ask in one line. |
| Which section | The heading in the copy the author reads. If their number and their description disagree, the description wins; say so in the first line. |
| Two headings fit | Live: ask once, quoting them. Prepared: take every one that fits and say so. |
| "Go through" or "walk" a section | Revise it with the author: plan, then parts. "Explain it" asks for no versions: explain only. |
| Which file, with no repository and several drafts | The one the record names. Else the one the author last named or was shown. Else the newest; say so in the first line. |
| "This paragraph", unnamed | The last paragraph quoted or shown. None: ask once, quoting the first words of each candidate. |
| Which version of the text | Fetch, then the head of the branch the destination builds from. It wins over an older note; say so if they differ. |
| Which working copy | A new one for this job, for example a new branch in a separate working tree. Never the shared copy coauthors edit. |
| Who wrote a section | The record, else the file's history. |
| The build command | The project's own instructions. |

Start of every job, in this order:
1. Find the project, the text and its version.
2. Make the working copy (not for an email).
3. Open or create the record ("The record" below).
4. Find or start the partner and brief it, unless "Who writes" gives the text to the lead alone.
5. The plan, as the unit file says. Live, a text of several sections whose depth the author has not named: the scope message first ([the walk file](references/author-walk.md)). Prepared: no scope message; the first message holds the plans and states the depth assumed per section.

## Starting and briefing the partner

Start the partner in whatever tool your team uses. Give it the whole text, the plan and the checklist.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | One standing partner for the whole job (TWO-WRITERS), not a new one-off run per part. |
| Which writer | A different model family from the lead, or a different person. One the author or the record named for this text comes first. |
| Where it writes | Only in the record folder. The brief names the exact path. |
| Late (its version is not back soon after the lead's passed its checks), or gone | Ask once. Record the gap. Live: the author does not wait. Send the lead's checked version alone, no mix, with the line "The partner's version is not back yet and will follow." If it arrives before the author chooses, send the full message. After: file it. Prepared: wait until the date the author was given, then as live. A partner that is gone: say so in one line and start a new one. |

- The lead never silently becomes the only writer.
- The brief holds: role, skill edition, the files to read, job, mode, type, "new", "rebuild" or "revision"; the author's words, word for word; the agreed plan, or "no plan agreed yet"; what to return now; the paths of the text and the record; the version of the text; how to reach the lead.
- Plans, drafts and checks travel as files. A short pointer is enough in a message.
- Neither writer opens the other's file for a part before its own draft is saved.

## Who writes, by type of text

| Text | Who writes prose |
|---|---|
| Paper section (the author's own, or a coauthor's part the author asked to rewrite). Grant section. Research proposal. Reference letter. Reply in a response letter. | Lead and partner, each its own version. The lead builds the mix. (TWO-WRITERS) |
| Email. Coauthor note. Cover letter or form text that restates approved prose. A response letter's opening and summary. | Lead alone, one draft. A partner joins only if the author asks for options. |
| An error with one right answer (a number, a reference, a term, a typo) | Lead alone, one fix. |
| A coauthor's section the author asks to check, or a part the author calls fine | Nobody rewrites. The partner audits. The lead verifies each finding. (MATERIAL-NOT-WORDING) |
| Any new or changed symbol, formula or formal statement | The mathematics author, as old/new pairs. The writers name the need; a helper pastes. |

## One message: what it holds, and how many versions

- Every message: the goal, the step, what comes next; the text; two or three lines on what was checked and what was not; one recommendation; at most one question. A job's first message may hold more: the author's questions, numbered, then the delivery question last.
- One part per message. A part is one plan line. Form: [templates/PART_MESSAGE.md](templates/PART_MESSAGE.md).
- The slots never change: Version 1 is the lead's, Version 2 the partner's, Version 3 the mix. No writer names are shown.

| Case | Current text | Versions |
|---|---|---|
| A part, live | In full. New text: "None: new text". | Three, one recommendation. (THREE-VERSIONS) |
| A repair the author asks for, of a paragraph not yet shown in this process | In full | Three. Version 1 is the smallest change that repairs the named fault. For a logic complaint: the recommended version's chain as bullets above the versions. |
| The author's own paragraph is sound, also when they ask to rewrite it | In full | Three. Version 1 is their paragraph with the smallest change. |
| The author names a fault, without giving words, in a version just shown or an approved part | Not repeated | One repaired version, by the lead. Say in one or two sentences what was wrong. Repair what was named; do not swing to the opposite. |
| The author gives replacement words | Not repeated | None. Apply the words exactly and record them. A number, citation or formal change in them gets the source check first; a mismatch is reported, never fixed silently. |
| The author says the intended effect on the reader is missing, calls a version just shown or an approved part or section bad and names no fault a repair can fix, or repeats a complaint on one part | None | None. The plan alone (the unit file). After agreement: three; Version 1 is the smallest change that meets the agreed plan. |
| The two writers converged | In full | Two texts, never one text twice: the converged text, and the current text with the smallest change. New text has no current text: show the converged text alone. Say "the two writers converged". |
| Prepared reading copy | As "Prepared work", step 3 | The author reads the recommended whole, and sees the three versions for any part they stop on and for each part where the writers disagreed. |
| A finding in a coauthor's section, or in a part the author calls fine | Quoted per finding | None. One findings message: "NO CHANGE", or one line per finding (class, place, old words, new words, the sentence in full). |

## Approval and applying

| The author's words | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Names a version they were shown ("Version 2", "the mix", "1 with the last sentence of 2") | Approval of that text. |
| Gives replacement words | Approval of exactly those words. |
| Approves by name a whole they were shown | Approved as shown. |
| Names findings of a findings message ("take 1 and 3", "all") | Approval of those findings' new words. Any other reply: ask which. |
| "Continue" | Approves nothing they have not seen. |
| One correction | Approves nothing else. |
| Any word a writer adds after the choice | Shown to the author inside its sentence first. |
| "Yes" to the delivery question | Go-ahead for this job and this text only, until its last part is delivered. |
| "Yes" to the sign-off question | Pending markup is removed for that section. (PREPARE-THEN-WALK) |

- The delivery question, one wording, under this one name in every file: "When you approve, may I put it into the shared copy, marked pending?"
- The sign-off question: "Do you sign off <the text> as it now stands? If yes, I remove the pending marks and deliver."
- The lead or one helper applies, after the choice is written in the record against the version shown.
- The reply to the author comes first. Applying, building and delivering run in the background.
- Each part has one state: prepared, shown, applied, delivered. Without a go-ahead, report "applied, not delivered", never "done".

| Step | Shared repository | No repository (grant file, letter) | Email |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where the lead works | The job's working copy. | The file the author reads; a copy is kept before each edit. | The draft. |
| Delivery question | Asked once per job, last in the first message. No answer: repeat it once. | None. | None. |
| Applied text | In the project's pending markup, if it has one. | No markup. | None. |
| Before delivery | Fetch, rebase, never overwrite others' work, build clean, the checks of [the build checks](references/paper-build-checks.md). | None. | None. |
| Delivered means | The destination shows it. Proof: the delivered file holds the approved sentence; the built page is looked at; the author is told to reload. | The file holds it; the author is told its path. | A draft with recipients and attachments, checked as rendered. |
| Sign-off | Asked once, when the job's last part is delivered. | Asked without its second sentence. A whole proposal: [the package file](references/grant-package.md). | None. |
| Leaving the team | Telling coauthors, sending to a journal, funder or anyone outside: the author's alone. | The same. | Sending is the author's. |

## The record: state across sessions

The record is a folder, `notes/writing/`, inside the project. It carries the state; no writer's memory does.
For a grant or a letter, the project is the folder of that grant or letter. For an email about a project, the record is that project's, with one queue sheet and one dated line in `DECISIONS.md`.

| File | Holds | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| `DECISIONS.md` | First line: job; skill edition; mode; manuscript path; destination; base version. Second line: any older record. Then, by part: the author's exact choice against the version shown, their corrections word for word, the delivery go-ahead, the part's state. Form: [templates/DECISIONS.md](templates/DECISIONS.md). | Lead |
| `CHECKLIST.md` | One line per fault the author has named, with a count and the parts it occurred on. Both writers read it before every part. Form: [templates/CHECKLIST.md](templates/CHECKLIST.md). | Lead |
| `plan_<section>.md`, `plan_<section>_partner.md` | Each writer's plan. | Lead; partner |
| `queue/<part>.md`, `queue/<part>_partner.md` | Lead's sheet: the card, the current text, each version, what happened to each piece, the send check record. Partner's sheet: its card, its version, its cross-check. Form: [templates/QUEUE_SHEET.md](templates/QUEUE_SHEET.md). | Lead; partner |
| `audit_<section>.md` | The audit of a coauthor's section. | Partner |
| `coauthor_note.md` | What changed in whose section; what needs their confirmation. Shown to the author in full with the sign-off question. Sending is the author's. | Lead |

- No record yet: the lead creates `DECISIONS.md`, `CHECKLIST.md` and the queue sheets from [templates/](templates/) and says so in the first message. The plan files follow [templates/PLAN_MESSAGE.md](templates/PLAN_MESSAGE.md). The audit file and the coauthor note have no form: plain lines, as their rows above describe.
- An older record elsewhere in the project: read it. Copy its checklist lines once. Name its path on the second line of `DECISIONS.md`. Parts it lists as not delivered are still open. Its delivery go-ahead does not carry over.
- A repository shared with coauthors: never commit the record. Exclude it locally.
- After a break or a lost context: read `DECISIONS.md`, `CHECKLIST.md`, then the queue. Resume at the first part not "delivered". Never ask the author what the record answers.

## The outside review

Bounded by SMALL-THINGS-YOURSELF.

| Text | Outside review |
|---|---|
| A paper section of more than one part that is new, or rebuilt (a new point, a new order of parts, or a big cut) | One, after assembly, with one comparison against [published examples](examples/published.md) |
| A revision (rewording inside the same plan) | None |
| One unit (an abstract, a conclusion, a repaired paragraph) | None |
| A grant, letter, email | None, unless the author asks |

- Prepared: the ready notice waits for it; if the author returns first, the notice says "outside review pending". Live: it never holds a part or the sign-off.
- A finding in approved text: name it to the author with a proposed repair. Never reopen it silently.

## Reports

At a section's end, or the end of a sitting that walked parts, send the author one full report: what was done, what was checked and skipped, what is open, what comes next. Part messages, single repairs, letters and emails need no report.

## Principles

### LEAD-WRITES  The writer the author talks to writes, now
Principle: in live work, the writer in the conversation drafts and shows text in that conversation. It does not go away to work and return later.

### TWO-WRITERS  A partner writes its own version
Principle: two independent writers, from different model families or different people, each draft the part, so the author can compare. Example: for a paragraph on why appointment reminders lengthened waits, one writer opens with a question, the other with the expectation that failed; the author keeps the second opening and the first ending.

### THREE-VERSIONS  Each part comes with three choices
Principle: the author chooses faster among three concrete texts (lead, partner, mix) than from one text and a description of alternatives.

### DRAFT-AHEAD  Prepare the next part while the author reads
Principle: while the author reads one part, both writers are already drafting the next, so the author never waits.

### MATERIAL-NOT-WORDING  In a coauthor's section, or a part the author calls fine, only material change
Principle: wording polish in such text costs goodwill and gains little; bring only material problems, or a plan to move material.
- Material is one of five classes: a wrong or inconsistent number; a claim stronger than its table or result; a contradiction with the revised sections; a broken reference or term; a step a reader cannot follow.
- A cut plan moves material to the appendix and deletes nothing.

### NO-FLEET  Work with the author, not around them
Principle: the job is the text with the author. Do not build large review machinery when the author is waiting for a paragraph.

### LOGIC-THEN-PROSE  Logic is walked before prose
Principle: the author's time goes first to the chain of the argument, then to sentences. The one-minute plan is the logic walk; the parts are the prose walk.

### PREPARE-THEN-WALK  Read a local copy, deliver at the end
Principle: in prepared work, the author reads a local reading copy and the text reaches the shared copy only after approval. A part the author has explicitly delegated is the exception.

### STAGED-REVISION  A coauthored draft is revised one link of the argument at a time
Principle: too many marked changes at once hide the argument. Revise one section, and within it one link of the chain, at a time.

### DIAGNOSE-FIRST  After coauthor pushback, diagnose before touching text
Principle: when coauthors object to a draft, first find the cause, working from structure down to wording, and agree a plan with the author before editing.

### SMALL-THINGS-YOURSELF  Small edits without mathematics need no outside reviewer
Principle: figure builds, wiring and word-level edits that change no symbol, formula or formal statement are done by the team without an outside review.
