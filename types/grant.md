# Type sheet: grant, research award, donor gift

## When this applies

- Any text of a grant, award or gift proposal.
- A call with a public page and a deadline is a grant. Money asked of a named donor with no call is a gift.
- A whole new proposal or a substantial revision: add [the grant package](../references/grant-package.md). A live walk of one section does not need it.
- Do not use [the writing runbook](../references/writing-runbook.md) for a grant. It is for papers.
- Everything this sheet does not state is in the core.
  - The team, the mode, who writes, how many versions, the message, approval, applying, delivery, the record, the outside review: [HOW_WE_WORK.md](../HOW_WE_WORK.md).
  - The plan, the card, the send check, the fresh reader, the fidelity read, a correction: [the unit file](../references/unit-plan-card-gate.md).

## The standard

General standard: [STANDARDS.md](../STANDARDS.md).
The examples below are invented. They describe an imaginary proposal to build an open record of rural water-pump repairs.

### GRANT-PERCEPTION  Decide what the funder's reader should perceive first
Principle: before writing, name what the reader should believe after reading. Then check that the text gets there. An abstract that lists activities has not decided this.

### GIFT-ORDER  Background, goal and vision, what we have done, use of the money
Principle: a gift proposal answers four questions in this order. What is the situation and its problem? What goal does the money serve? What has the team already done? What will the money pay for?
Scope: gift proposals. It is the default order for other grants too.

### WHY-NOW-CONTRAST  The background is a contrast that leads to what the money buys
Principle: a background that only lists facts does not persuade. Set what is possible against what stands in the way, and end on the missing piece the money supplies. The order of the first two is the writer's choice.
Example (invented): "Last spring one village carried its water four kilometres for eleven weeks, because a worn seal on its pump went unreported. The repair took a mechanic one hour. Faults of this kind recur across the district, since nobody writes down what broke or what fixed it. This gift pays for that written record, a shared repair log every district mechanic can read and add to."

### PLAN-STEP-BY-STEP  Any plan, goal or vision is a build the reader can picture
Principle: describe a plan as a sequence: the foundation first, then each step built on the last. Keep it broad, with a figure; it is not a timetable.

### PAST-GRANTS-CARRY  Carry the author's program, arc and verified facts
Principle: a new grant continues the author's earlier grants. Reuse their arc and their checked facts. Do not borrow another funder's opening or start from a framing the author never used.

### ONLY-NEEDED-FACTS  Keep only the facts the perception needs
Principle: every fact must serve the belief on the card. Inventory counts (the number of interviews transcribed or pages scanned) rarely do.

### CONTEXT-FIRST  Give the outside reader the context before the claim
Principle: the funder's reader does not know the field. State where we are before stating what we found.

### GRANT-REGISTER  Sharp, professional, readable
Principle: show sharp insight in professional language. Use the neutral professional term for the people studied. No hedging, no jargon, no filler. Fewer words beat more.
- Each paragraph has one job, with enough sentences to develop it. Three to seven sentences is a guide against fragments, not a numeric gate.

### NO-UNTESTED-PROMISES  Promise nothing untested or unagreed
Principle: do not report results that have not been built or tested. Do not name a partner who has not agreed in writing.

### AUTHOR-AGENDA  Name the work as the author's research agenda
Principle: describe the work as the research agenda of the people who will do it. Name an institutional program, center or initiative only when one formally exists.

### GRANT-BOLD  Bold the parallel points the reader must carry
Principle: a funder's reader skims. Mark in bold the few parallel points that reader must retain, so that the bold alone gives the argument.
Scope: grants and gifts. Papers and academic proposals use no bold ([STANDARDS.md](../STANDARDS.md)).

## Roles of the units in this type

A section's role comes from its heading and job, never its number.

| Role | Its job | First sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Why now / background | A contrast: the opportunity, what blocks it, and the missing piece the money supplies. It ends on that piece. | A concrete case or fact, not a general claim about the field. |
| Goal and vision | The exact research and where it leads, with a figure. | GAP. Trial: the foundation, and what research on which questions. |
| Track record | The reader concludes that this team already does the work well and that others rely on it. Findings first, credentials last, no inventory counts. | The claim the record supports. |
| Plan / milestones | A sequence, foundation first. Each step has an output and its user. Broad, with a figure; not a timetable. | The foundation step. |

### Craft notes (trial)

These are working defaults. They may be bent.

| Role | Its job |
|---|---|
| Abstract | Stakes, why now, our answer, compact credibility, what the award does, who uses it. |
| Methods | Purpose and decision problem before machinery. |
| Deliverables | Parallel items with informative bold leads, dates, users, measured outputs. |
| Use of funds | Each line funds named work. Each plan step gets its use. |

A closing section about the principal investigator may be requested. Its standard is a GAP.

## Workflow, step by step

Only the steps a grant adds. Every other step is in the core files named above.

1. Lead, first: open the author's past grants, the funder's current requirements and this grant's record.
2. A new call: send the fit message ([grant package](../references/grant-package.md), "Fit"). See "Different from the general case".
3. Map the author's order onto the funder's headings. The call's headings win. The author's order runs inside them. Show the mapping in the plan of a whole proposal.
4. The plan of a whole proposal names the funder's reason to care. A one-section plan carries neither.
5. A proposal title is broad and plain, normally without a colon.
6. A contrast background is planned as two parts: the opportunity and what blocks it; then the missing piece the money supplies.
7. A short text that restates approved prose (portal summary, budget justification, cover letter) follows its row in [HOW_WE_WORK.md](../HOW_WE_WORK.md). A short text that makes a new argument is a section.
8. Figures: the lead designs what each says. Whoever renders works from that design. Each figure gets one card line: the reader's question and the belief.
9. The grant's decisions file also names the current file, its render command and how to view it.
10. Before each edit, keep a copy with a name that cannot collide, such as `<file>_pre_<unit>_<UTC>.md`. Never overwrite one. Write its path beside the author's choice in the decisions file.
11. After applying: render, look at every page image, then swap the new file in atomically.
12. A prepared whole proposal: the readiness test and the ready notice ([grant package](../references/grant-package.md)).

## Different from the general case

| Decision | General case | A grant |
|---|---|---|
| The first message of a new call | The plan. | The fit message. It asks the author to choose the arc and to give the facts only they can give. Live: each section's plan follows once the author agrees the arc. Prepared: the plans go with the fit message, labelled "on the recommended arc, not agreed", and the ready notice lists the arc as open. |
| The fresh read of a prepared whole | The fresh reader gets the passage and answers the three questions of the unit file. | The readiness test ([grant package](../references/grant-package.md)). The reader gets only the rendered PDF and the call's description of its reviewers. |

Use, in order: the same role in the author's submitted grants; rejected drafts as what to avoid; the role line above.
No accepted example: write from the role line and tell the author so. Never fall back to a paper role.

## Checks before the author sees it

These are the rows the send check runs for a grant ([the unit file](../references/unit-plan-card-gate.md), "The send check").
On a plan message for a whole proposal it runs only the GIFT-ORDER row. On a plan for one section: none.

| Standard | Who | How | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| GRANT-PERCEPTION | Fresh reader when one is due; else the lead | Reads the unit; says what it now believes | Its words match the card's perception |
| GIFT-ORDER | Lead | Lists the sections against the author's order, inside the funder's headings | The mapping is in the plan. The call fixes the headings: each of the four sits under one, and the order holds inside each. It fixes none: background, goal and vision, what we have done, use of the money |
| WHY-NOW-CONTRAST | Fresh reader, always | Reads the background alone; names the contrast and the thing being sold | Both named; the thing sold is what the gift funds |
| PLAN-STEP-BY-STEP | Fresh reader, always | Retells the steps in order and the output of each | Order and outputs correct; a figure exists |
| PAST-GRANTS-CARRY | Lead | Lists the past-grant arc and facts carried | List in the record; opening not another funder's |
| ONLY-NEEDED-FACTS | Lead | For each fact, names the card line it serves | No fact without one; no inventory counts |
| CONTEXT-FIRST | Fresh reader when one is due; else the lead, reading as an outsider | Marks any sentence it could not follow | None marked |
| GRANT-REGISTER | Lead | Runs [card_check.py](../scripts/card_check.py) (a hit is a prompt to inspect, not a fault); searches for jargon, hedges, em-dashes; reads each paragraph for its one job | Every hit inspected and justified or fixed; no fragment paragraph |
| NO-UNTESTED-PROMISES | Lead | Holds each partner, dataset and result against written evidence in the application folder | Evidenced, reworded as prospective, or on the open-decisions list |
| AUTHOR-AGENDA | Lead | Searches "program", "center", "initiative" and institution names | Only the author's research agenda named |
| GRANT-BOLD | Lead, on the render | Reads only the bold | It carries the argument; no ornament |
| Numbers | Lead | Number trace (unit file); budget numbers to their line in the budget file | Every number traced |
| Approved text | Lead | Diffs the file against the copy kept before the edit | Only the approved span changed |
| Whole proposal: readiness | Fresh reader | The readiness test ([grant package](../references/grant-package.md)) | Each answer matches the intended belief; nothing material missing or wrong |
| Whole proposal: established style | Lead | Compares the rendered pages with the author's accepted grant | Material differences recorded and resolved |

## When the author says it is bad

- What to do the same turn, and how to repair: [the unit file](../references/unit-plan-card-gate.md). What enters the skill: [MAINTENANCE.md](../MAINTENANCE.md).
- The author rejects a frame that a role line above gives: the agreed plan's frame governs that unit. Say so in the partner writer's brief.

## Gaps

This sheet is thin in places.
- GAP: first-sentence frames for goal and vision, abstract, methods, deliverables, use of funds. Meanwhile use the trial lines and say so in the plan.
- GAP: the section about the principal investigator. Meanwhile use an example the author approved; say no standard exists.
- GAP: whether GIFT-ORDER binds every grant. Meanwhile the default in its scope line.
- GAP: [card_check.py](../scripts/card_check.py) on grant prose. Meanwhile use its rise option only on a unit planned as a rise.
- GAP: no accepted gift proposal is on file as a whole.
